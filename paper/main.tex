\title{Recall or Discovery? A Falsifiable Instrument for\\
Law Induction with Language Models}

\author{Marco Vanadia}
\institute{Independent Researcher, Warsaw, Poland\\
\email{marco.vanadia@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
When a language model shown planetary data answers ``Kepler's first law,'' it is
impossible to tell discovery from recall: the data is in its training set. We
present an instrument that separates the two. Laws are induced from three kinds
of worlds---a historical dataset (Tycho Brahe's Mars observations), synthetic
planets propagated by a flight-dynamics service, and perturbed worlds where the
textbook law is false---under paired blind and labeled protocols. The model
proposes free-form mathematical expressions; a deterministic harness fits their
constants and judges them on held-out data, split in time rather than
interleaved, because a law is a claim about extrapolation. On sparse historical
data, naming Mars improves prediction 3.2-fold (memorization quantified); on
clean synthetic data the gap vanishes. Shown its own residuals, the model
reproduces Kepler's 1602--1605 path from a near-miss harmonic to the exact
conic. Every result carries a replayable receipt.
\keywords{Falsifiability \and Scientific discovery \and Large language models
\and Symbolic regression \and Memorization \and Causal inference}
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}

``Self-improving agent'' and ``AI scientist'' are, as usually demonstrated,
unfalsifiable claims: demonstrations exhibit successes, benchmarks are chosen
after the fact, and---for famous scientific results---the conclusion is in the
training corpus, so a correct answer carries no information about the process
that produced it. Popper's demarcation applies to the demonstrations
themselves~\cite{popper}: an experiment that cannot fail is empty, and an
answer that may be recalled cannot count as discovered.

This paper contributes an \emph{instrument} rather than a system: a protocol
that measures, in the same units, how much of a language model's ``discovery''
is inference and how much is memory. Two refusals anchor it:
\begin{equation}
A \vdash c \;\;\not\Rightarrow\;\; \vDash c
\end{equation}
no agent is the judge of its own claims---that an agent \emph{derives} a
conclusion never entails that the conclusion \emph{holds}; and
\begin{equation}
P(Y \mid \mathrm{do}(X)) \;\not\equiv\; P(Y \mid X)
\end{equation}
seeing is not doing~\cite{pearl}---the two coincide exactly when confounding is
absent, which is what may never be assumed. The first axiom dictates the
architecture: the model proposes, and a deterministic harness fits, scores, and
refuses. The second dictates the data: worlds where looking and acting
disagree, and worlds where the remembered answer is wrong.

Concretely, we make four contributions. (i)~A \textbf{law grammar}: the model
writes an arbitrary expression over a small operator algebra with free
constants; a parser (never \texttt{eval}) and a deterministic fitter turn the
proposal into a falsifiable object (Sect.~\ref{sec:instrument}). (ii)~A
\textbf{recall/inference measurement}: paired blind and labeled protocols over
historical, synthetic, and law-breaking worlds, with a scale-invariant
observable that anonymization cannot destroy (Sect.~\ref{sec:worlds}).
(iii)~A \textbf{time-split judge}: laws are scored by fitting on the earliest
observations and predicting the latest, after an interleaved holdout was shown
to certify shapes whose futures are wrong by an order of magnitude
(Sect.~\ref{sec:judge}). (iv)~\textbf{Measurements} (Sect.~\ref{sec:results}),
among them: a 3.2-fold recall advantage on Tycho Brahe's Mars data that
vanishes on clean synthetic planets; the reproduction of Kepler's
harmonic-to-ellipse refinement in two turns of residual feedback; a one-shot
recovery of the $T \propto a^{3/2}$ law from eight anonymized synthetic
planets; and the first observed \emph{refusal to refine}, on residuals that
really were noise.

Everything is replayable: propagated arcs are committed with their generating
requests, fits are deterministic, and only the proposals themselves were live.

\section{Related Work}

Symbolic regression discovers analytic laws from data by search: from
evolutionary approaches~\cite{schmidt} through physics-informed methods such
as AI~Feynman~\cite{aifeynman}, sparse regression over derivative libraries
(SINDy)~\cite{sindy}, and modern engines such as PySR~\cite{pysr}. Our harness
solves a smaller fitting problem than these systems; the contribution is
orthogonal---the measurement of \emph{where a proposal comes from}. A symbolic
regressor cannot recall Kepler; a language model can, and recent
LLM-as-scientist systems~\cite{aiscientist} inherit exactly this ambiguity.
Benchmark-contamination studies quantify memorization on text tasks; we
quantify it on law induction, where a scale-invariant observable (an exponent)
lets the same question be asked with and without the disguise. The axioms,
the receipt discipline, and the gated self-evolution framework this instrument
builds on are established in a companion system paper~\cite{companion}; no
result is reported in both.

\section{The Instrument}\label{sec:instrument}

\subsection{Laws as Expressions}

A proposed law is a string over the grammar
$\{\,+,-,\times,/,\ \cos,\sin,\exp,\log,\sqrt{\ },|\cdot|,\mathrm{pow},\
\pi,\ x_1,\ c_1..c_4\,\}$, parsed by a recursive-descent parser---never
evaluated as code---with the free constants $c_i$ fitted by the harness using
deterministic multi-start Nelder--Mead: the same expression on the same data
yields the same constants in every process. The model chooses the \emph{shape}
of the law; the arithmetic belongs to the machinery. Node count is reported as
a complexity measure alongside every score. Offering the model a menu of named
families is itself an information leak (Sect.~\ref{sec:results}); the grammar
replaces the menu.

\subsection{Worlds}\label{sec:worlds}

\textbf{Historical.} Five positions of Mars triangulated from Tycho Brahe's
observations by the method of \emph{Astronomia Nova}~\cite{kepler}, transcribed
to the arc-minute and verified against physics by test: the triangulation must
land on an orbit with Mars's shape, or the transcription is refused.
$\mathtt{intervene()}$ raises---nobody intervenes on a planet.

\textbf{Synthetic.} Planets that have never existed, with seed-derived
Keplerian elements, propagated by an Orekit-based flight-dynamics service and
committed as fixtures with their full generating requests. Real physics, no
textbook entry: recall is impossible by construction.

\textbf{Law-breaking.} The same machinery under force models where the
textbook answer is false: an oblate primary precesses the ellipse (no closed
$r(\theta)$ exists), and atmospheric drag---a dissipative, non-central
force---decays the areal rate that Kepler's second law says is constant.
Notably, oblateness alone cannot break the second law: $J_2$ is axially
symmetric, so equatorial angular momentum is conserved. On these worlds,
memory is a measurable liability.

\textbf{Anonymization.} Blind arms rescale both variables by arbitrary
constants and strip all provenance. Under a power law the rescale changes only
the prefactor: \emph{the exponent is scale-invariant}, so the disguise cannot
leak the one number that is the discovery.

\subsection{The Judge}\label{sec:judge}

Every law is scored twice: on an interleaved holdout (interpolation) and by
fitting on the earliest 60\,\% of observations and predicting the final 20\,\%
(extrapolation). The second split exists because the first was shown to
certify impostors: on a decaying orbit, a hyperbolic-tangent ``law'' scored
0.06\,\% relative error interleaved and collapsed 22-fold on the time split
(Table~\ref{tab:judge}). A law \emph{extrapolates} if its future error stays
within $3\times$ its interleaved error \emph{or} within $10^{-3}$ of the
data's own scale, whichever is more generous. Both bounds are necessary, and
the second was found by the first's failure: the criterion began as a pure
self-ratio, and the \emph{true} shape failed it---a law that interpolates
near-perfectly can carry a future error eight times its interleaved error that
is still 0.08\,\% of the data. A self-ratio punishes excellence. The judge's
own first version was falsified by its calibration data, and that record is
kept.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

All numbers replay offline from committed fixtures and receipts; only the
proposals were live (Gemini~3.6/3.5 via typed JSON schemas, every exchange
recorded whole: prompt, answer, answering model, timing).

\subsection{Recall, Quantified}

On Tycho's five points, blind and labeled arms saw identical rows and
committed a prediction for the same held-out point before scoring
(Table~\ref{tab:recall}). Naming Mars improved the prediction 3.2-fold. On a
synthetic planet (138 clean points), the blind prediction error fell to
$1.4\times10^{-4}$ of the data scale and the model could name nothing---there
is nothing to name. Memorization is a crutch for sparse data, not a substitute
for physics.

\subsection{The Menu Is a Fingerprint}

When the blind protocol offered four named families including
$r = p/(1+e\cos\theta)$, the model said ``Keplerian'' even blind. With the
grammar replacing the menu, the recognition vanished: the same synthetic
planet became ``a rotating sensor measuring distance to an off-center
target''---an orbit, described by someone who has never heard of one. The leak
was in the offered formulas, not the data. Conversely, in the third-law
experiment the model named ``Child--Langmuir or Kepler's third law'' from
eight anonymized $(a, T)$ pairs---recognition \emph{earned} from a measured
exponent, which is in the data. The instrument distinguishes leaked
recognition from earned recognition.

\subsection{Astronomia Nova in Two Turns}

Free-form and blind, the model proposed
$c_1 + c_2\cos(2\pi x_1 + c_3)$---the first-order conic, the nearly-right
shape at which Kepler stood in 1602---leaving a $400\times$ systematic
residual it could not see and the harness could. Shown those residuals, it
observed their doubled frequency, matched it to ``the second-order Taylor
expansion of the polar equation for an ellipse,'' composed
$c_1/(1 + c_2\cos(2\pi x_1 + c_3))$, collapsed the held-out error 448-fold,
recovered the injected eccentricity ($c_2 = 0.060$, truth $0.06$)---and only
then said ``Keplerian orbit.'' Recognition arrived when the evidence forced
it.

\subsection{The Third Law, One Shot}

Eight synthetic planets ($a \in [0.47, 2.20]$\,AU), each propagated
$\sim$1.3 orbits; period measured by wrap time, axis by geometry---never
injected. The log-log slope across the fleet is 1.507 before any agent looks.
Blind, the model ran its own log-log analysis, composed
$c_1\,\mathrm{pow}(x_1, c_2)$, and the fit returned $c_2 = 1.515$ with
0.4\,\% relative held-out error.

\subsection{The Second Law, and a Refusal}\label{sec:secondlaw}

The swept-area instrument measures Kepler's conserved quantity directly:
areal-rate drift of $-0.003\,\%$ on a two-body planet and $-0.008\,\%$ on a
two-body low orbit (the law, measured), against $-1.107\,\%$ under
atmospheric drag (the law, broken)---140$\times$ the control's drift with one
force-model field changed. Under the time-split judge (Table~\ref{tab:judge}),
the model's saturating proposals from an early run are refused, a rational
shape whose second-order expansion contains the true behavior is certified,
and---shown residuals that really were noise---the model \emph{declined to
refine}: ``oscillatory behavior without a clear systematic trend.'' A system
that can only ever refine cannot be trusted when it does; the refusal is what
makes the refinements informative.

\section{Limitations}

Every live arm is $n{=}1$: the variance of one-shot proposals is unmeasured,
and all proposals come from one model family. The judge certifies
extrapolation over the recorded horizon only---the rational shape of
Sect.~\ref{sec:secondlaw} has a wrong asymptote twenty windows past the data,
where no finite evidence reaches; a law survives its tests and is never proven
by them. The propagation service's heliocentric clock runs a consistent
$13.06\times$ fast (an effective $\mu \approx 170\times$ solar); exponents
survive, constants do not, and the defect is pinned by test rather than
hidden. Anonymizing \emph{time} remains open for real datasets: our synthetic
elapsed times carry no identity, but Tycho's dates would. Finally, comparisons
against symbolic-regression baselines are future work; the instrument, not the
fitter, is the contribution.

\section{Conclusion}

The question ``can a language model do science?'' is unanswerable while
discovery and recall are indistinguishable. We built the scale that weighs
them apart, calibrated it on the one dataset where memorization is guaranteed,
and watched the needle move: 3.2-fold on sparse famous data, nothing on clean
anonymous data, negative where the textbook is wrong. Along the way the
instrument disciplined its own builders---the judge's first criterion was
falsified by its own calibration and the corrected version kept the record of
its failure. \emph{Ex probatione propria non sequitur veritas}: from one's own
proving, truth does not follow. Including ours.

\subsubsection*{Acknowledgements.} The system described here was built
meta-agentically: an AI pair-engineer proposing at speed, a human judging,
refusing, and redirecting---neither trusted as the judge of its own claims.
